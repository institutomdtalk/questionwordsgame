Introduction

What is the verb to be?

The verb to be is the most important verb in English. It is used to describe identity, professions, nationalities, characteristics, states, and locations.

In the present simple, it has three forms that change according to the subject:

SubjectFormContraction
Iam'm  →  I'm
Youare're  →  You're
He / She / Itis's  →  She's / He's
We / You / Theyare're  →  We're / They're
Note: Contractions are very common in spoken English and informal writing. In formal contexts, the full form is preferred.
02

Affirmative Form

Affirmative Sentences

Subject  +  am / is / are  +  complement

In affirmative sentences, the verb to be follows the subject directly. No other verb is needed.

I am a teacher.
Eu sou professor(a).
She is very talented.
Ela é muito talentosa.
They are from Brazil.
Eles são do Brasil.
The book is on the table.
O livro está sobre a mesa.

Notice how is is used with he, she, it — all singular third-person subjects.

03

Negative Form

Negative Sentences

Subject  +  am / is / are  +  not  +  complement

To form the negative, simply add not after the verb to be.

Full FormContraction
I am notI'm not
You are notYou aren't  /  You're not
He / She / It is notHe isn't  /  She's not
We / They are notWe aren't  /  They're not
I'm not ready yet.
Eu ainda não estou pronto(a).
They aren't at home.
Eles não estão em casa.
Attention: I am not has only one contraction: I'm not. The form amn't does not exist in standard English.
04

Interrogative Form

Questions with To Be

Am / Is / Are  +  Subject  +  complement  ?

In yes/no questions, the verb to be moves before the subject. No auxiliary verb is needed.

Are you ready?
Você está pronto(a)?
Is she your sister?
Ela é sua irmã?

For WH-questions, the question word comes first:

WH-word  +  am / is / are  +  Subject  ?
Where are you from?
De onde você é?
What is your name?
Qual é o seu nome?
05

Key Uses

When to Use To Be

UseExample
Identity / NameI am Maria.
ProfessionHe is an engineer.
Nationality / OriginShe is Italian.
CharacteristicThe film is boring.
State / FeelingWe are tired.
LocationThe keys are on the desk.
AgeMy brother is 25 years old.
Remember: In Portuguese, ser and estar are two separate verbs. In English, to be covers both — so the choice is always about which form to use, not which verb.
